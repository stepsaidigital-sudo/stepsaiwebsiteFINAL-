The problem with vanity metrics

"1,000 messages handled this month" sounds impressive and tells you almost nothing. Was it 1,000 easy questions, or 1,000 people the agent quietly failed and nobody noticed? A message count can't answer that. An outcome can.

The numbers, from a real account

The dashboard above is a real account's numbers, not an illustration built to look good. 412 conversations. 358 answered solo. 24 meetings booked. 30 passed to a human. Every number on this page is from a real account or clearly marked as an example — we don't publish numbers we can't show you.

What it handled alone

358 out of 412 conversations closed without a human touching them — not "the agent replied," but the conversation actually reached a resolution. That's the number that answers "is this actually working," not the raw message count.

The dashboard updates in real time

A plain-English weekly summary lands in your inbox too, so you don't have to go looking for these numbers to know how the week went.

What it's worth

24 meetings booked isn't a vanity stat — each one is a calendar slot that got filled without anyone on your team touching a scheduling link. That's the difference between "the agent was busy" and "the agent produced something you'd have paid a person to produce."

Where it's weak

30 conversations got passed to a human — and that number matters just as much as the other three. Every one of those is a question the agent couldn't answer safely, which points straight at the page or document that needs fixing, not at a vague "AI isn't perfect" shrug. A dashboard that only shows wins is hiding the most useful part of the report.

Why we publish it this way

A summary, not a spreadsheet: how many conversations closed without you, what it's worth in bookings and updates, and where it's still weak — those are the only three things worth checking a dashboard for. Everything else is noise dressed up as a metric.

Those 24 booked meetings and 30 handovers are all outcomes generated by conversations that started somewhere specific — a workflow, a channel, an inbox. If you haven't seen how those upstream pieces work yet, One Inbox and Workflows are where they're covered.
