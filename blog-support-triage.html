The problem with scattered channels

If your team is growing, you've hit the same wall every support team hits: customers message you wherever is easiest for them — WhatsApp at night, Instagram DMs after seeing an ad, the website chat widget mid-checkout. Someone still has to open all three, read every message, and decide who answers what.

A simple "is this still in stock?" ends up in the same queue as a refund dispute. Whoever reads messages first ends up doing the sorting, not the support — and the customer waits either way.

Enter One Inbox

One Inbox is exactly what it sounds like: every conversation, from every channel, lands in a single list — tagged by where it came from, and already marked with what happened to it. The screenshot above is a real inbox view. Nothing is invented for this post.

Notice the last column: Resolved, Booked, Lead saved, With Priya. That's not a message count — it's an outcome. The agent already did something with each conversation before a human ever had to look at the list.

Step 1: every channel, one place

Connecting a channel takes minutes, not an integration project — WhatsApp Business API, Instagram DMs and your website's chat widget all route into the same inbox. The WA/IG/Web tags in the list above are the actual source of each message, not a mockup label.

We only publish what we can show

Every screenshot in this post is a real inbox and dashboard view, not a mockup drawn to make a point. If a number or status looks unglamorous, that's because it's real.

Step 2: the agent still answers first

One Inbox isn't a better way to read messages faster — it's what's left over after the Support Agent has already answered most of them. Order status, policy questions, "is this in stock" — all of that gets resolved before it ever needs a human glance. What lands in front of your team is mostly what the agent flagged, not everything.

Step 3: handover, when it's actually needed

Meera's refund question above didn't get a scripted answer — it went straight to Priya, tagged With Priya so nobody else picks it up by accident and nothing falls through twice. That's the actual design principle: the agent handles what it can resolve safely, and hands off cleanly — with the full conversation history attached — the moment a real judgment call is needed.

What this changes

Nothing about this replaces your support team — it removes the sorting step they were never actually hired to do. The team spends its time on the refund, the escalation, the judgment call. The password reset, the stock check, the "what are your hours" — those never reach a human queue at all.
